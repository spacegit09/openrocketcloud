Motor Designer · solid rocket motors

Design a solid rocket motor for OpenRocket

Work out a sugar-propellant motor with Richard Nakka’s SRM method: grain, chamber pressure, thrust curve and motor class. The results match his SRM_2023 spreadsheet and METEOR, and one click adds the motor to OpenRocket.

See how
Calculation

The same numbers as SRM_2023 and METEOR

The motor is worked out by JSRM, the Java version of Richard Nakka’s SRM_2023 that METEOR runs, laid out like his spreadsheet. In our tests, the results match SRM_2023’s cells and METEOR’s.

  • The same sheets as SRM_2023: Data and Kn, Pressure, Performance, Nozzle Design, Output and Propellant data
  • Kn, chamber pressure and thrust through the burn, with every line of the calculation in a table
  • Erosive burning, combustion and nozzle efficiency, and nozzle erosion, as SRM_2023 counts them
  • Give the throat diameter, or a target pressure and let the throat be sized for it
Grains

Seven grain shapes, drawn to scale

Choose a hollow cylinder (BATES), rod and tube, C-slot, finocyl, moon burner, star or end burner. The grain is drawn to scale as you type, and the calculation follows its burning surface as it burns back.

  • One grain or several identical segments
  • Inhibit a BATES grain’s core, ends or outside; the ends of other shapes
  • Cells take numbers or formulas, like a spreadsheet: =1.5*25.4
  • A clear message when a grain does not fit the chamber or cannot burn
Performance

Thrust curve, impulse and motor class

The Performance sheet gives the thrust curve, maximum thrust, total impulse, delivered specific impulse and the motor’s class, from Richard Nakka’s measured burn rates for each propellant.

  • Eight propellants: KNDX, KNSB fine and coarse, KNSU, KNER, KNMN, KNPSB and KNFR
  • Charts of Kn, pressure and thrust, in metric with pounds alongside
  • A warning when the pressure goes beyond the propellant’s measured burn-rate data
  • Nozzle sizes and a drawing of the nozzle from its angles and expansion ratio
OpenRocket

Into OpenRocket in one click

“Use in OpenRocket” adds the motor to your custom motors, so it appears in OpenRocket’s motor selector in every design. You can also download it as a .eng file for desktop OpenRocket.

  • Thrust-time data written from the calculated curve
  • Name, maker, delays, size and hardware mass for the motor file
  • A red dot on the Output tab shows the details still needed
  • After a change, click it again to replace the motor in OpenRocket
Your motors

Saved to your account

Each motor is saved with its sheets and listed with its thrust curve and class, so you can come back to it, copy it to try a change, or rename it.

  • Stored encrypted, like your designs
  • Search your motors by name
  • Runs in the browser: nothing to install, on a Chromebook too

Live co-editing

Edit together, live.

Open a design and your teammates are right there with you. Every change appears for everyone the moment it is made — no files to pass around, no “final_v7.ork”.

  • Avatars show who is here and which part each person has selected
  • The header tells you what just changed: “Joon Lee edited Trapezoidal Fin Set”
  • Invite people by their Google email as Can edit or Can view
  • Viewers can look around freely; nothing they touch changes the design
Edit history

Every change, on the record.

The history lists every change in plain words — “Nose cone · Length: 32.4 cm → 35.6 cm” — with who made it and when. Mistakes are never permanent.

  • Undo one person’s changes, keeping everything changed since
  • Restore the whole design to how it was at any moment
  • Undone changes stay listed, and can be redone
  • A downloadable copy is saved every 10 minutes while people edit
Your designs, everywhere

Always saved. Always with you.

Your designs live online and open on any computer you sign in to, with a drawing of each rocket on its card. Start from one of OpenRocket’s examples, or upload the .ork files you already have.

  • Saving is continuous — there is no Save button to forget
  • Download any design as an .ork file that opens in desktop OpenRocket
  • Upload custom motors (.eng, .rse) once; everyone on a design gets them
  • Duplicate a design to try an idea without touching the original
Built-in engineering tools

The calculations you used to do in a spreadsheet.

Added to OpenRocket’s Tools menu and to every fin set — working from your design, so the answers change when the rocket does.

Recovery

Parachutes and ejection charges, sized for your rocket.

Tools → Recovery works out what each parachute brings down from the design itself — its stage after burnout, the other devices open with it, and the air at your landing site.

  • The diameter for the descent rate you want, and the landing energy (competitions cap it at 75 ft·lbf)
  • One click sets the parachute in the design
  • Black powder for the bay at your pressure, measured from the design
  • Allows for how full the parachute packs the bay, from published ground tests (NARCON 2025)
  • Checks your shear pins break with a 2× margin
Ballast

Nose weight, worked out.

Tools → Ballast finds the nose weight that gives every motor configuration at least the stability you want, using OpenRocket’s own mass and stability calculation.

  • Choose the stability you want and where the weight sits in the nose
  • See each motor configuration’s stability now, and the weight it needs
  • Add it to the nose cone in one click; change or remove it the same way
  • Every change can be undone, like any other edit
Fin flutter

Catch fin flutter before your fins do.

Every fin set gets a Flutter tab: the speed at which its fins would start to flutter, from their shape, thickness and material — checked against every simulation, in that flight’s own air.

  • Flutter speed from Martin’s NACA boundary, as corrected for rockets
  • The margin for each simulation, and where in the flight it is closest
  • Clear warnings when a flight goes faster than its fins can take
  • Typical stiffness for OpenRocket’s materials, or enter your own
3D and 3D printing

Made for rockets you print.

See the rocket in 3D in any browser, bring the parts you print into the design, and export fins the way they really are.

3D view

See it in 3D, in any browser.

Desktop OpenRocket draws its 3D views with OpenGL, which a browser can’t run. Rocket in Cloud redraws them from OpenRocket’s own 3D shapes, so the 3D Figure, 3D Unfinished and 3D Finished views work on any computer, with your paint and decals.

  • Pick 3D Figure, 3D Unfinished or 3D Finished under the rocket figure, as in OpenRocket
  • Drag to turn the rocket, right-drag to move the light, scroll to zoom
  • Click a part to select it, shift-click to add more, double-click to edit it
  • 3D Figure shows what is inside: parachutes, bulkheads, motor mounts and your printed parts
3D-printed parts

Bring in the parts you print.

Tools → Import 3D model (STL) turns a printed part, such as an avionics sled, a bulkhead or a camera shroud, into real mass and balance, so the stability and every simulation account for it. Aerodynamics still come from OpenRocket’s own shapes.

  • Set the file’s units, which axis runs along the rocket and which end faces the nose
  • Printed walls and infill, solid, or a hollow shell — or enter the mass you weighed
  • Add it as a new part, or give its mass and centre of gravity to the part you selected
  • Drawn as the model itself in the 2D and 3D views, shared with your team and kept in downloaded .ork files
Fins

Airfoils and fillets, drawn and exported.

OpenRocket uses each fin set’s cross section in its drag and its root fillets in its mass, but leaves both out of its 3D shapes. Rocket in Cloud puts them in: rounded and airfoil fins, and fillets on both sides of every fin that run out at the fin’s edges.

  • See the real fin shape in the 3D view before you print
  • 3D exports (OBJ) include the cross section and the fillets, ready for your slicer
  • Fillets take the radius set in each fin set’s dialog
  • Fixes OpenRocket 24.12’s “3D printing” export, which failed on some designs such as its pod examples

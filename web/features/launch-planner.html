Launch Planner · real weather, real ground

Know where it lands — before you launch.

The Launch Planner flies your OpenRocket design hundreds of times in NOAA’s forecast for your field and launch time, and shows on a satellite map where it is likely to come down.

How it works
How it works

Three steps. About a minute.

Pick your rocket

Choose a design and one of its simulations. OpenRocket works out the rocket’s aerodynamics, motors, mass and recovery, right in the page.

Set the field and time

Click the map to place the pad, pick a launch time up to 16 days ahead, and set the rail’s length and angle.

Fly it 500 times

Each flight varies what you can’t control. In seconds you see where 50, 95 and 99% of them land.

Real weather

The forecast for your field — not a guess.

Winds and air come from NOAA’s GFS forecast for the exact spot and hour of your launch, at every height your rocket flies through.

  • The ensemble forecast: each flight flies in one of NOAA’s 31 forecasts, so the landing area is as wide as the weather is uncertain that day
  • Or a fixed spread on the main forecast, the same margin every day
  • Wind speed, direction and the ensemble’s range at every height
  • The rail can tilt into the wind, as a range would set it
Monte Carlo

Every uncertainty, flown.

Real flights never go exactly as simulated. The planner varies the motor’s thrust, drag, mass, the parachutes, the rail’s aim and the ejection delay — then flies the rocket hundreds of times.

  • Where 50%, 95% and 99% of flights land, as ellipses on the map
  • Apogee with its range, flight time, top speed and landing speed
  • The farthest landing, and the flight in the forecast as it is
  • A replay of the flight in 3D over the satellite map
Accurate where it counts

OpenRocket’s physics, a thousand times faster.

OpenRocket computes your rocket’s aerodynamic tables in the page; a six-degree-of-freedom flight simulation then flies them in the browser’s background threads.

0.5%apogee agreement with OpenRocket on its single-stage examples
10 mlanding point agreement with OpenRocket’s own simulation
~1,000×faster than running OpenRocket’s simulation itself
31NOAA ensemble forecasts flown, each by its share of the flights

On real ground

Landings meet the real terrain, from open elevation data, not a flat plane at the pad’s height.

Save your plans

Keep a plan — settings, forecast and results — and open it again later, as a card with a picture of where it landed.

Straight into the logbook

Bring a plan’s predicted flight into your logbook and see how close the real flight came.

Predictions, not a substitute for your range safety officer or your club’s safety code. Single-stage rockets for now.

Rocket in Cloud Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Rocket in Cloud (rocketincloud.com) runs OpenRocket, the open-source model rocket simulator, in your web browser and saves your work online. This policy explains what information Rocket in Cloud collects, how it is used, who can see it, and the choices you have. “We” and “us” mean the people who run Rocket in Cloud.

The short version

  • We collect only what the app needs to work: your name and email address from Google, and the things you create.
  • Your designs, motors, saved flights and logbooks are stored encrypted. In the app, only you and the people you share them with can open them.
  • We don’t sell your information, show ads, or use your information for advertising.
  • You can delete your work at any time.

Information we collect

From your Google account. When you sign in with Google, we receive your name and email address. We use them to sign you in, to show your name to you and to the people you work with, and to let others share designs and logbooks with you by email. We never receive your Google password, and we don’t access your Gmail, Drive, contacts, calendar or any other Google data.

What you create. Your rocket designs (.ork files), with their edit history and saved copies; custom motor files you add; Launch Planner plans you save; and logbooks, with their flight details (such as date, time and location), notes, flight data, photos and videos. We also keep the list of people you share each design or logbook with, and their roles.

Basic usage information. Base44, which hosts Rocket in Cloud, automatically records basic usage events. These include which page was opened, how long a visit lasted and which website sent you. Base44 also keeps standard server logs, such as IP addresses and request times. We use this information only to understand how the site is used and to fix problems.

How your work is stored

  • Designs, motors, saved flights and logbooks are stored encrypted in Rocket in Cloud’s database. They don’t appear in readable form in the hosting dashboard. The app decrypts them only to show them to you and to the people you share them with.
  • Photos and videos in logbooks are stored as private files. Only people who can open the logbook can view them, and the app gives them links that expire within an hour.
  • OpenRocket itself runs in your browser, so your simulations and edits are computed on your own device.

Who can see your information

People you share with. When you share a design or logbook, the people you add can see it, along with your name and email address. Viewers can look at it, and editors can also change it. When you edit a design together, everyone in it sees each other’s names and edits, and the edit history shows who made each change.

Services that run the site. Each of these handles data under its own privacy policy:

  • Base44 hosts the site, the sign-in, the database and file storage.
  • Google provides sign-in (Google Privacy Policy).
  • Leaning Technologies (CheerpJ) provides the Java runtime that OpenRocket runs on. Your browser downloads it from their servers.
  • Launch Planner map. The map’s software comes from jsDelivr, satellite images from Esri, and ground heights from Amazon Web Services’ open terrain data. Your browser requests these directly, so those services can see your IP address and the area of the map you are looking at.
  • Wind forecasts come from the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). Our server requests them for your launch site’s location, so NOAA doesn’t see your IP address or your account.

Us. As the operators, we can see the list of accounts (name, email address and when each joined) and the usage information described above. We don’t look at your work unless you ask us to help with a problem, or unless we must do so to protect the service or to comply with the law.

Legal requests. We may disclose information if the law requires it.

We don’t sell your information, and we don’t share it for advertising.

Information stored in your browser

The site keeps a few things in your browser: your sign-in session, a copy of OpenRocket’s program files so it opens faster next time, and small settings such as your preferred units. Rocket in Cloud uses no advertising or tracking cookies.

Google user data

We use the information we receive from Google only to sign you in and to identify you to the people you work with, as described above. Rocket in Cloud’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping and deleting your information

  • Your work is kept for as long as your account exists, unless you delete it.
  • You can delete any design, motor, saved flight, logbook, photo or video in the app at any time, and the app removes it from Rocket in Cloud’s database. Base44 may keep a deleted photo or video file in its storage, but the app can no longer open it. Copies may also remain in Base44’s backups for a limited time.
  • Leaving a design or logbook that was shared with you removes your access to it.
  • To delete your account and everything in it, contact us (see below).

Security

Connections to Rocket in Cloud are encrypted (HTTPS), your saved work is stored encrypted, and access is checked on our server each time. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information and will act promptly if a problem is found.

Children

Rocket in Cloud is for students, hobbyists and teams of all kinds. You need a Google account to sign in, so you must meet Google’s minimum age for an account in your country, or use an account managed by your school or parent.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page. If the changes are significant, we will also let you know on the site.

Contact

For questions about this policy, or to ask for your account to be deleted, open an issue on Rocket in Cloud’s GitHub page. Issues there are public, so don’t include your email address or other private details. We will arrange a private way to confirm it is you.

This policy covers rocketincloud.com and rocketincloud.base44.app. It doesn’t cover copies of Rocket in Cloud that other people run themselves from its source code.
